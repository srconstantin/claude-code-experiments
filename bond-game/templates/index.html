📐 Bond Basics — What Is a Bond?

A bond is a loan you make to a government or company. They promise to pay you regular coupons (interest) and return your face value (principal) at maturity. But the bond's market price changes daily based on interest rates.

💡 Key insight: Bond price = the present value of all future cash flows, discounted at the current market yield.

⚖️ The Price-Yield Seesaw

Bond prices and yields move in opposite directions — always. When new bonds offer higher yields, existing bonds must drop in price to compete. The relationship isn't linear — it's convex, which is great news for bondholders.

💡 Rule of thumb: Price change ≈ −ModifiedDuration × ΔYield. But convexity makes reality a bit better than this estimate.

⏱️ Duration — Your Bond's Interest Rate Sensitivity

Duration measures how much a bond's price changes when rates move. Think of it as the bond's "sensitivity to rate shocks." A bond with duration 8 loses ~8% if yields rise 1%.

💡 Two factors increase duration: longer maturity and lower coupon rate. Both push more cash flow further into the future.

📈 The Yield Curve — A Map of the Bond Market

The yield curve plots yields for bonds of different maturities. Its shape encodes what the market expects about future rates and the economy. Traders make billions watching it shift.

💡 The 2s10s spread (10Y yield minus 2Y yield) is the most watched indicator. Negative = inverted = recession signal.

💳 Credit Risk & Spreads

Not all bonds are equal. Corporate bonds must pay a spread above risk-free Treasuries to compensate for default risk. The riskier the issuer, the higher the spread, the lower the price.

💡 Spread = total yield − risk-free yield. A BBB bond at 1.4% spread pays 1.4% above Treasuries. Junk bonds (BB and below) can trade at hundreds of basis points of spread.
