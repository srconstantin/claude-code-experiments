# Notes on the typography album

Source: the fourth Google Photos album (https://photos.app.goo.gl/dRCTo2bFjgmSmTLNA), 77 images. These notes focus on the unusual display faces with curvilinear forms. Image numbers are their order in the album (0-based).

## The individual faces

**NYMPH (13).** High-contrast capitals on a classical skeleton. Strange in specific, local ways:
- The left stroke of N leaves the baseline as a hairline that loops back on itself before becoming the thick diagonal.
- Y's arms are two heavy wedges that taper to points.
- M is built from leaning crescents.
- The bowl of P hangs from the stem by a hairline.
- H's crossbar is a swash that curls past the right stem.

Every thick stroke is where you'd expect it in a classical capital, so the word reads at once. All the invention is in the hairlines and in which end of a stroke tapers. Terminals are sharp thorns, never balls.

**959 (15).** Ultra-black Art Nouveau numerals.
- The counters are teardrops and almonds cut into a heavy mass.
- At the top and bottom of each figure, the stroke splits into two or three tendrils. These flare outward and curl, so the serif is a *bifurcation of the stroke itself*, bracketed with liquid curves.
- The white shapes (counters, the gaps between tendrils) are drawn as carefully as the black.

Of everything in the album, this is the closest to "ornament where the serifs go, made of branching strokes".

**blandglos / filantrop / Endorfina (56).** A heavy geometric display face built on one strange rule. Many bowls are split down a vertical: one half solid black, the other half a hairline arc that completes the circle.
- Stems end in sharp diagonal cuts.
- Contrast is reversed in places, with hairline verticals and heavy horizontals.

It is strange because one unusual rule is applied *consistently*, so the result reads as a system, not a whim.

**Ar1427 (60).** A liquid, calligraphic sans with letters made of very few strokes.
- Each stroke is a long wedge or ribbon: it starts as a hairline and swells to full weight, or the reverse.
- Terminals are pointed flicks, and curves are long, elongated S-shapes.
- The construction is brush-like, but every edge is perfectly crisp and taut: no wobble, no texture, no rounded marker ends. The tension of the curves is what makes it elegant instead of casual.

**HELLO NEW WORLD (67).** A Didone-like skeleton with straight hairline stems, where *some* strokes are replaced by long tapering liquid loops:
- the E's arms
- the L's foot
- the W's first stroke
- the D's bowl, which overruns and crosses the stem

The loops cross into neighboring letters, so a word becomes a ligature. The beauty comes from the contrast between rigid verticals and fluid sweeps.

**frivole (66).** Low contrast and restrained.
- Strokes don't quite join: there are small gaps at the junctions, almost stencil-like.
- Terminals are wedge cuts, and the e's bar is a diagonal flick.
- The f's hook is a forward-leaning blade.

It is elegant because it holds back: generous spacing, few strokes, and unjoined nodes that give it air.

**AGE OF (69).** High-contrast hairline serif capitals.
- Letters interlock and overlap: the G's tail runs under the E, and the A's leg forms a heavy flat wedge.
- One letter, the O, becomes a solid black disc in a square.
- Hairline rules above and below frame it like an inscription.

Strangeness comes through ligature and scale contrast within a word.

**"I might be at the super market" (41).** A monoline hairline Latin alphabet built on a Hangul-like structure. Letters are assembled from straight lines and single curves, and the skeletons are unfamiliar but internally consistent. Elegance comes from the uniform hairline and lots of white space.

**anna karenina (63).** A high-contrast lowercase with stencil breaks and looped joins, stacked vertically. The a and k use loops where you expect straight strokes.

**PACER (3), ATTEMPT (22), FOLD (23).**
- PACER: wide hairline capitals with sharp spurs.
- ATTEMPT: heavy letters whose curves are cut by circles, like ink traps, giving mushroom-like terminals.
- FOLD: outlined, interlocking geometric forms.

These are less curvilinear but share the "one strange cut, applied everywhere" logic.

**Art Nouveau whiplash: Machinedrum (35), Orane (75), cumbia (32), purple "Paris" poster (33), AGAG (34), Cyrillic cover (31).**
- **Orane:** botanical calligraphy, with flame- and leaf-shaped tapered strokes radiating from the letters. The type grows into the ornament.
- **cumbia:** swollen ribbon letters of constant weight with loops.
- **Purple poster:** letters stretched into long, tangled, liquid verticals.
- **AGAG:** glassy, inflated, high-contrast swashes.
- **Cyrillic cover:** condensed monoline letters with Art Nouveau terminals and inner hairline doubles.

In all of these, ornament and letter are one continuous stroke.

**Nike XI wall (50).** Huge black wedges tapering to needle points: letters as thorns or claws, with extreme contrast and no curves left round.

## What they have in common

1. **Organic does not mean wobbly.** Every one of these has crisp, taut, vector-perfect edges. The curves are long and few, with tension, like a Bézier with two well-placed handles. The biomorphism is in the *shapes* (wedges, teardrops, loops, splits), never in the *edges*. Hand-drawn wobble or rounded marker ends read as casual or comic.

2. **Extreme contrast, allocated by a rule.** Hairlines against heavy strokes. Which strokes are heavy is unconventional (reversed stress in Endorfina, wedge taper in Ar1427) but systematic.

3. **Tapers end in points.** The typical terminal is a wedge or thorn, or a flick that thins to a hairline. Round blobs appear only when the whole face is inflated (cumbia, 959's lobes).

4. **The ornament is part of the stroke, not added to it.**
   - In 959, the serif *is* the stroke splitting.
   - In HELLO NEW WORLD, an arm *becomes* a loop.
   - In Orane, the letter *grows* into leaves.

   Nothing looks attached. Where a strange element appears, it is continuous with the stroke it comes from, with a smooth hairline transition and no seam.

5. **One or two strange rules, applied everywhere.** Split bowls, unjoined nodes, swash replacements, bifurcating serifs. Consistency turns oddness into a style.

6. **The key strokes stay put.** Legibility is protected by keeping each letter's identifying thick strokes where they belong (the diagonal of N, the stems of H, the bowl of P). Liberties are taken with secondary strokes: hairlines, feet, arms, tails.

7. **White space is drawn.** Counters, the gaps between tendrils, and stencil breaks are shaped as deliberately as the black.

8. **Scale and spacing.** Hairline faces get generous space and are set large. Heavy faces are set tight and interlock. Ligatures and overlaps turn words into logos.

9. **Dignity comes from classical scaffolding.** The most beautiful ones (NYMPH, HELLO NEW WORLD, AGE OF, Daphne) keep classical proportions and a serif-like logic. The strangeness reads as refinement, not a joke.

## What this means for Neuron

Why my earlier attempts looked silly, measured against the album:
- wobbly hand-placed curves
- round marker-like ends
- leaf-shaped strokes that taper the same way at both ends, so everything looked like a brush
- ornaments stuck on as separate little appendages, at the wrong scale and with visible seams

Principles to carry forward:
- **Skeleton:** constructed and taut, not hand-drawn. Few curves, long, with tension. Keep each letter's identifying strokes in their classical places.
- **Contrast:** high, with a consistent rule for which strokes are heavy.
- **Terminals and serifs:** where classical serifs and terminals go, *the stroke itself* turns into compound-like structure, as in 959 and Orane. A stem foot could split into the branches of a real fork or sprig, with the branches growing continuously out of the stem's own width and thinning to points. The compound shape should be large enough to read as itself, and fused to the stroke without a seam.
- **One rule:** pick one compound behavior (bifurcation from forks, a side-branch from sprigs, or a row of barbs from combs) and apply it at every serif position, so the strangeness is systematic.
- **Edges:** the compound shapes should be redrawn crisp and taut, keeping their topology and proportions (where they branch, how far, which way they curl) but not the jaggedness of the trace.
