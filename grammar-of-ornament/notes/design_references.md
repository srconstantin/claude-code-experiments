# Design references: notes on structure

Notes from three reference albums Sarah shared (2026-10-21). Only the graphic
design was considered: posters, album covers, packaging, websites, magazines and
books. Apparel, paintings and photography were skipped. References look like
`1-010`, meaning album 1, image 10, in download order.

- Album 1: https://photos.app.goo.gl/jZfnBiYgndTGaa8M8 (142 images; the most
  "creature / ornament / generative")
- Album 2: https://photos.app.goo.gl/8B4uJn8hzt7VUGSa8 (88; fashion-heavy,
  with editorial and identity work)
- Album 3: https://photos.app.goo.gl/8NJZr4TXMZeMCgve7 (90; colour-heavy,
  playful, pixel and type work)

## Why the first cover studies felt conservative

- **Layout.** All four used the same layout: one picture, one title, a polite
  margin, warm beige paper, and a classic serif or grotesk. They read as
  reissues of old science books.
- **What the references do instead:**
  - **Extreme scale contrast:** a huge figure next to type a few points high.
  - **Systems of small type:** metadata, coordinates, lists, captions, all in
    quantity.
  - **Type and image interact:** type is invaded by ornament, wrapped around the
    picture, built from the same marks, or cut out of it.
  - **Structure is shown:** hairline grids, callout boxes, leader lines,
    numbered markers.
  - **Bolder colour:** black and white plus one acid accent, chrome, or flat
    saturated fields. Beige is rare.
- **Typography in the references.** Most pair two kinds of type:
  - a neutral wide grotesk or monospace at small sizes, for the information;
  - something strange at large sizes: blobby, calligraphic blackletter, pixel,
    stencil, or a hairline serif at display size.

## Ways to structure graphic elements

### A. Figure as emblem or frame

1. **Symmetric emblem around a void.** `1-010` (S10) is a black, mirror-symmetric
   ornament with a radiant eye at its centre. The date sits big, in a thin serif,
   in the empty lower half; a small paragraph is set on an arc above.
   Related: `1-020`, `1-045` (patch), `2-031`, `1-062`, `1-069`.
   - **Our version: imperfect bilateral symmetry.** Put a motif on one side and a
     *sibling*, mirrored, on the other. The two halves nearly match but aren't
     copies.
2. **Quadrant or lattice tiling of one creature.** `1-034` places four dragons in
   a rhombus kaleidoscope across the top half; the bottom half is blank paper
   with a tiny title. Related: `1-107`, a rhombus lattice of eyes that shrinks
   down the page.
   - **Our version:** one motif family in a 2×2 or rhombic tiling, each cell a
     different sibling, with tiny type and a large empty area.
3. **Ornament as a frame or cartouche.** `1-137` crops a photo to a baroque
   silhouette. `1-092` surrounds a picture with a thin linework border, and
   `1-096` floats a tiny image in a large textured field.
   - **Our version:** use a motif's *outline* (the clean mask) as the window onto
     its own feature, with a frieze of the same family as the border.

### B. Type and image interacting

4. **Ornament invading type.** `1-067` splits the page into a white half and a
   black half. Black-and-white linework overruns huge grotesk letters, and the
   small information sits in grid cells.
   - **Our version:** let motif ink grow over a big word, with the black and
     white inverting across the split.
5. **Text as frame.** `2-032` runs the title around all four edges of an
   engraving, rotated, mirrored and set in a custom face. `3-061` circles big
   numerals with arrows and text on paths; `1-010` sets text on an arc.
   - **Our version:** type set along the outline of a motif, or along a frieze's
     baseline.
6. **Display type from the material.** These references build display
   letterforms from their own visual material:
   - `2-024`: a giant custom glyph-blob runs along the bottom, next to a
     monospace column repeated down the page.
   - `3-060`: huge blobby type over pixel-stepped colour fields.
   - `2-083`: stencil type over a faint linework wallpaper, with symmetric blue
     silhouettes.
   - `3-071`: a folded type-specimen poster whose letters are made of pipe
     segments.

   We postponed a typeface, but the same effect is possible without one: a few
   huge letters cut *out of* a feature, so the letters are filled with it.

### C. Annotation: the specimen plate

7. **Callout boxes and leader lines.** In `1-057` a jewelled figure is
   surrounded by thin-ruled boxes holding close-ups and tiny monospace data. In
   `1-097` a list of names has leader lines running to small thumbnails, with
   ghostly scribbled lettering behind. `1-113` and `1-114` overlay hairline
   UI rectangles on a photo; `3-089` has an orb with dotted leader-line labels.
   - **Our version (the strongest fit):** one big feature or motif, plus
     magnified insets of *the same* feature at its own fine scale, labelled with
     layer, channel, scale and coordinates in monospace. It is honest about what
     the images are (instruments looking at a network), and it keeps to one
     source.
8. **Terminal, ASCII and data aesthetic.** Examples: `1-009`, `1-118`, `1-053`,
   `1-077` (figures drawn in characters or line fragments), `1-044` (a book
   spread with monospace text and pixel-grid images), `3-044`, `3-086`.
   - **Our version:** draw a deep motif out of tiny copies of a small mark, the
     way ASCII art builds a figure from glyphs.

### D. Fields, grids and registers

9. **Grid of variations.** `1-049` is a grid of texture tiles, `3-005` a grid of
   pixel figures, `3-087` a mosaic of stamped tiles, `3-049` a quilt.
   - **Our version:** a sheet of one motif's siblings, set as a type-specimen or
     catalogue page with numbers. It shows "the same but different" outright.
10. **Horizontal registers.** `2-046` (Kerning) has fat horizontal bars with
    gradient fills, thin vertical grid lines, and tiny type. `3-057` repeats a
    leaf shape in rows over hairline stripes, with text on curves and an
    information block at the bottom.
    - **Our version:** friezes as the page's *structure*, not decoration: each
      band is a register holding one row of the system, with the type in the
      gaps.
11. **Pixel steps and quantization.** Examples: `3-060`, `3-058` (pixel blobs
    with labels), `3-083` (a QR-like border around a sphere), `2-082`, `3-053`.
    - **Our version:** the features are 224 px images, so showing their pixel
      grain at a large size is honest. It also reads as a contemporary style.

### E. Surface and material

12. **Blind emboss and relief.**
    - `1-060`: six tiny embossed sprigs on olive stock, and nothing else.
    - `2-022`: embossed linework on terracotta.
    - `1-112`: a dark relief creature with small numbered circles.
    - `1-093`: a chrome bas-relief.
    - `2-062`: a relief magazine cover.

    **Our version:** emboss motif silhouettes (a single source) on coloured
    stock, with numbered markers. Coloured, not beige.
13. **Chrome, glow and halo.** Examples: `1-101`, `1-102`, `1-117`, `1-122`,
    `2-031`, `2-044`. These are less native to our material. A soft glow behind
    a dark figure is about as far as it should go.

### F. Composition

14. **Huge empty space with tiny type.** Examples: `1-034`, `1-096`, `2-047`,
    `1-031`. Most of the page is empty and the type is very small.
15. **Collage of hard cut-outs.** `3-088` scatters cut-out shapes across the page,
    behind a big grotesk headline set right across it. `2-085` splits the ground
    into two colours, with a black silhouette grafted onto engravings.
    Related: `1-120`, `3-074`.
    - **Our version:** many sibling silhouettes from one family, scattered and
      overlapping like cut paper, under one big headline.
16. **Split or diptych.** `1-067` splits black and white; `2-085` splits
    yellow-green and blue; `2-039` is two panels.
    - **Our version:** the same motif shown positive on one side and negative on
      the other.
17. **Dense editorial layering.** `2-021` lays chunky chrome-gradient letterforms
    over newspaper columns. `1-123` (Aurora) packs dates, codes, small text and
    rotated labels around an object. Related: `2-010`.

## Palettes seen

- Black and white only, or white with one acid accent. Examples: `1-010`,
  `1-034`, `1-067`, `2-029`.
- Orange-red with lime (`2-046`), or lime with blue (`3-060`).
- Pale lilac or blue with black (`2-083`, `3-089`, `2-031`).
- Chrome or silver greys (`1-093`, `1-070`).
- Saturated flat fields laid next to each other (`3-057`, `3-059`).
- Warm terracotta and olive are used for relief and emboss pieces (`2-022`,
  `1-060`).

## Next studies this suggests (each from one source)

1. **Specimen plate.** One deep motif, large, plus three or four magnified insets
   of the same feature, hairline callout boxes, and monospace metadata. Black,
   white and one accent.
2. **Imperfect emblem.** A motif and its mirrored sibling around a central void,
   with a large hairline-serif date and an arc of small text, after `1-010`.
3. **Kaleidoscope.** Four siblings in a rhombic tiling across the top half,
   blank lower half, tiny title, after `1-034`.
4. **Invaded type.** A huge grotesk word on a black/white split, with the
   motif's ink growing over it, after `1-067`.
5. **Registers.** A full page of friezes as horizontal bands, with the
   information set in the gutters, and acid colour, after `2-046`.
6. **Cut-paper collage.** Twenty siblings scattered as silhouettes under one
   headline, after `3-088`.
7. **Pixel honesty.** A feature shown at its native 224 px as big visible
   pixels, with a pixel-stepped edge and a small grotesk, after `3-060`.
8. **Blind emboss on colour.** Six embossed siblings on olive or terracotta, and
   nothing else, after `1-060`.
