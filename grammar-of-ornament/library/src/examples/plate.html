A network trained to recognize photographs has, in one of its deeper layers, a channel that responds to something we would call an arch: ribs that spring from a base, bend over, and come down again, filled with a scaled, carved texture. Ask it to draw what it is looking for, and this is what it draws.

The plate is the network's drawing cut to one figure, its tones pressed to pure black and white so that it can be printed, carved, or set beside type. Nothing in it was drawn by hand except the line around it.

“Ribs that spring from a base, bend over, and come down again.”

What makes it ornament rather than picture is repetition: the same arch appears three more times in the same print, at different sizes and turned a little, and the network's vocabulary of marks is small enough to read.

  • Combs: a spine with teeth along one side.
  • Feathers: barbs on both sides of a spine.
  • Hatching: short parallel strokes that fill a band.

The colours are one ConvNeXt feature's own: the paper is a tint of the biggest patch of colour in it, and the ink, the rules and the pull quote are colours present in the picture.
