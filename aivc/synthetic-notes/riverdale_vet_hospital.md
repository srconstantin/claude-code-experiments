# RIVERDALE ANIMAL HOSPITAL

site visit 3/3, bronx ny location. drove up from financial district w/ amanda.

owner: dr. michele varghese-petersen, DVM. 56. wants to slow down (3 days/wk instead of 5). owns 100%. no kids in vet med. her husband (also a doctor, MD) is pushing her to extract value while corporate consolidation is still paying premium multiples.

practice metrics
- single location, multi-doctor (4 DVMs + 1 part-time)
- 4,200 sqft, 5 exam rooms, 2 surgery suites
- ~14,500 active patients (visit last 24 mo)
- '25 revenue: $5.9M
- ebitda: ~$1.42M (24% margin, healthy for GP companion animal)
- service mix: wellness/preventive 38%, sick visits 24%, surgery 17%, dental 8%, boarding/grooming 7%, retail 6%
- avg invoice $312

doctor productivity
- michele: $1.45M production (3 day reduced sched coming)
- dr. ng (senior associate, 12 yr tenure): $1.32M
- dr. abrams (4 yr): $1.08M
- dr. peters (2 yr): $740K (still ramping)
- PT dr martinez: $310K

decent bench but michele is still the rainmaker for high-acuity surgery referrals.

acquisition context — VERY important
companion animal vet has been the hottest small-business rollup category of the last decade. mars veterinary health (banfield + bluepearl + vca), nva (national veterinary associates / jab holdings), thrive, ethos, sound vet — all paying 11-15x ebitda for quality multi-doctor practices in metros. some of those premiums have softened post-2023 but still robust.

michele's asking 12x = $17M. that's at the high end but the multi-doctor depth and NYC location justify it somewhat.

deal structure she wants
- 70% cash, 25% rollover into our pet-health platform, 5% retention bonus paid yearly over 3 yrs
- 3 day/wk employment agreement at $480k base for 5 yrs (effectively a long earn-down)
- 5 yr non-compete w/ 10 mile radius (NY non-compete law is squirrelly, need to verify this is enforceable)

competition
- mars + nva have both made multiple bids on this practice over last 2 yrs per michele
- they're at 12x. so are we, if we want it.
- distinguishing factor isnt the price, it's the cultural fit pitch + how much control the seller retains in operations

risks
- ASSOCIATE RETENTION. when an indep vet hospital sells to a corp, associates often leave w/i 24 months. dr. ng (the $1.32M producer) is the biggest risk. has been there 12 yrs, has equity-feeling without equity. losing him cuts ebitda 25%+.
- NYC real estate. michele rents the space, lease has 4 yrs left + one 5 yr option. landlord could be a problem. need to verify lease assignability.
- vet labor market. nationally vet techs are 18-25% short of demand. an in-the-bronx hiring market is tougher still.
- pet insurance penetration is climbing — good for utilization but compresses pricing power
- corporate veterinary backlash — some clients (and some associate vets) explicitly hate corp ownership. could be a churn driver if poorly handled
- GLP-1 for pets is a real thing (slentrol etc historically, new generation coming). impact on obesity-related visits could shift the case mix.

upside
- multi-doctor + multi-revenue-stream (boarding, grooming, retail) means there are real synergy opportunities w/ our platform
- nyc density. plenty of more practices nearby to roll up. michele's relationships in nyc vet community are an asset.
- the 4,200 sqft facility is underutilized (only 5 exam rooms, could add 2 more). capacity expansion is real

thesis
companion animal vet rollup is well-trodden, well-priced, well-understood. there's still alpha in metros where local relationships matter and the big corps cant just plug-and-play. michele's practice fits.

main concerns are deal-specific: associate retention is critical and the lease situation is a hidden risk.

would offer 11x normalized ebitda = $15.5M, w/ a 2-year retention pool of $2M structured to keep dr. ng and dr. abrams (the two strongest associates).
