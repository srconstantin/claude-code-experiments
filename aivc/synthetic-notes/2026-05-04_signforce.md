# SignForce Manufacturing — visit notes 5/4

manufacturing shop in elk grove village IL (chicagoland). makes interior + exterior commercial signs — channel letters, lightboxes, ADA wayfinding, pylon signs, vehicle wraps. mostly serves national franchise chains (multi-unit restaurant + retail).

met w/ kevin and donna obrien (married couple, founded 2003, ~62 and 60). their son matt (35) is operations manager. broker is JD Merit.

ops
- 38,000 sqft fabrication facility, owned
- 47 employees: 22 fabricators (welders, fabricators, CNC operators), 8 install crew (2 trucks), 6 sales/PM, 5 design/permit, 4 admin, kevin + donna + matt
- equipment: 2 fiber laser cutters, 1 waterjet (older), 6 CNC routers, a vacuum forming line for plastic faces, paint booth, big inventory of aluminum + acrylic stock

financials
- ttm rev: $11.2M
- ttm gross profit: $4.4M (39.3% — solid for the trade)
- ttm ebitda: $1.78M (15.9%)
- addbacks: kevin's $290k salary (replace at $150k production mgr), donna's $140k (she does AR/AP, replace at $75k bookkeeper), 2 cars, a boat, country club, ~$60k total in personal pass-thru
- normalized ebitda: ~$1.95M
- working capital is huge — they carry $1.4M in AR (typical for the industry, 65-75 days DSO from national franchise customers) and $680k in raw inventory. cash conversion cycle is 95 days.

customer concentration is the big issue here
- top 3 customers: 41% of revenue
  - chipotle (national rollout sign provider, ~22% of rev)
  - planet fitness (~12%)
  - a private equity owned QSR chain (~7%)
- top 10: 68% of revenue
- ~140 active customers total but the tail is small

contracts: most are not exclusive but they have "preferred vendor" status. franchise national construction managers control the spec. switch costs are real but not insurmountable — chipotle could move 60% of their sign work elsewhere with one decision.

competition
- Federal Heath Sign (texas, huge), persona signs, identity group — all bigger national fabricators
- regional shops like signforce — dozens across the country
- signforce's edge: chicago location (good for midwest franchise rollouts), strong PM team, fast turnaround (avg 18 days from PO to install vs 30+ industry)

real estate
- they own the building, want sale-leaseback at close. ~38k sqft @ $7.50/sqft NNN = $285k/yr rent. building is worth maybe $4.2M. they want $4.5M, separate transaction.

valuation thoughts
- specialty manufacturing in this category trades 5.0-6.0x ebitda. customer concentration penalty would pull to 4.5-5x.
- kevin asking 5.5x normalized = $10.7M, plus the building
- i'd offer 4.75-5x = $9.3-9.75M with a 12mo earnout tied to keeping chipotle revenue stable

key risks
- CHIPOTLE — single customer, 22% of revenue, one buyer decision. need to understand the relationship deeply. has it been renewed recently? who's the relationship owner — kevin or matt or someone else?
- working capital. when we close, the cash AR balance gets paid down to seller via the NWC peg, then the new entity has to fund the working capital growth. for a $11M revenue biz w 95-day CCC, that's $2.8M of WC. need to size revolver appropriately.
- skilled tradespeople. they have an open req for a welder for 4 months. apprenticeship pipeline isnt great in chicago.
- permitting work is a quiet liability. they do a lot of municipal permit pulling and small mistakes can mean re-fabrication losses.
- matt as successor — he's 35, been in the biz 9 years, but kevin is still very much running the show. need to evaluate matt's actual readiness, not the sales pitch version.

upside / thesis
- sign manufacturing is a fragmented industry. ~3,500 small shops nationally, no real consolidator other than federal heath. there's a rollup opportunity, but it's harder than home services because national-account relationships are harder to transfer.
- if we could anchor signforce + 2-3 add-ons in different geographies, we could offer national franchise customers a true multi-region single-vendor solution. that's a real value prop to chipotle, planet fitness, etc.
- BUT: customer concentration is going to make every lender ask hard questions. and PE-backed roll up of an industry hasn't really happened in signs the way it has in hvac or pest. there's a reason.

leaning a soft yes if the price is right. would not pay $10M+ for this.
