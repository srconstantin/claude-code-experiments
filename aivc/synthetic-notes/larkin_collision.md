# larkin collision — two location auto body — 1/26

shop visit canton + akron ohio. met joe larkin (sole owner) at the canton location. he gave us the walk thru of both shops in one afternoon.

quick facts
- two collision shops, both i-car gold class certified
- canton: 14 stalls, opened 1998
- akron: 9 stalls, acquired 2014
- ~38 employees combined (techs, estimators, parts, front)
- LTM rev $9.1M, LTM ebitda $1.42M (15.6% margin, decent for collision)
- joe is 59, ready to slow down but not retire entirely. wants a 3-yr deal w meaningful rollover

DRP relationships (direct repair program — insurance company referrals — this is the heart of the biz)
- state farm "select service" both locations
- geico "auto repair xpress" canton only
- progressive "service center" both
- usaa both
- allstate good hands canton only

DRP volume = 71% of revenue. that's the moat AND the risk. carriers consolidate vendors every few years.

competition
- caliber collision (big national platform, PE-backed via hellman & friedman) — has 4 locations in greater cleveland but none in canton/akron specifically
- crash champions, gerber collision/boyd — both in the region
- joe estimates ~50 independent collision shops in his service area, mostly mom-and-pop

joe's pitch for why he's good
- DRP scorecards are top quartile (faster cycle time, lower supplement rate, fewer comebacks)
- showed us the state farm scorecard — 87% on time, 4% comeback. genuinely strong
- aluminum certified both locations (rare for indep)
- ~30% of his work is now EV which is growing fast and most indep shops cant do

addbacks/normalization issues
- joe's wife on payroll @ $72k, doesnt come in. straight comp addback.
- joe's salary $310k, ops mgr replacement market is $145k. addback the diff.
- shop rent — joe owns both buildings personally, charges himself $18k/mo combined which is above market by maybe $4k/mo. need to set market rent at close.
- "supplies" line has a lot of personal stuff in it. need QoE to wash through.
- normalized ebitda probably $1.55-$1.65M after addbacks (vs his claimed $1.7M)

valuation read
collision multiples have come off the peaks. caliber paid 7-9x in 2020-22, now 5-6x for tuck-ins. joe wants 6x. i'd come in at 5.25-5.5x normalized = $8.2-8.6M EV plus the real estate.

real estate — both buildings, ~28k sqft combined, joe wants to do sale-leaseback. canton building is fine, akron building needs roof + paint job, estimated $180k deferred maintenance. need to credit that.

risks
- DRP concentration. if state farm pulls the relationship (their post-helene crackdown on shop scorecards is making the rounds), we lose 25%+ of revenue overnight
- aluminum tooling is expensive and getting more expensive. EV battery handling cert is harder still.
- tech shortage. one master tech can do $500k of repair labor per year, two left to caliber in 2025 already
- insurance industry has been pushing labor rates down (the famous "prevailing rate" fights), squeezing margins industry-wide

thesis
collision is a fragmented industry that rolls up well but the big platforms (caliber, crash champions, gerber) have been hoovering up the prime targets for 5+ years. larkin is a strong indep but we're competing w/ those platforms on price. the OH market specifically still has some white space and joe is well-positioned. i could see this as a 2-shop tuck-in for our broader collision platform or a small standalone bolt-on.

not super excited but the unit economics are real. would do it at the right price.
