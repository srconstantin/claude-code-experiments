agency basics

- ~$3.4M revenue (commissions + fees) ltm

commercial mix is the gem. personal lines is a money loser at this size after carrier compression.

retention

valuation
- typical lower-mm p&c agency trades at 2.0-2.75x revenue or 7-9x ebitda. EBITDA basis = $7.4-9.5M, revenue basis = $6.8-9.4M. so call it $7.5-9M valuation range.

- counter at 2.5x = $8.5M plus 18mo earnout on retention

deal structure
- 70% close, 25% in 18 month earnout tied to commercial retention, 5% rollover equity
- 5 yr non-compete + non-solicit on henry, 3 yr employment agt as "agency president" $250k base
- nephew (will keller) gets a producer agreement w defined book equity over 5 yrs

competition in market
- regional brokerages (BRP group, hub international, baldwin) — all PE backed, all active in southeast acquisitions, all paying full prices
- some local agencies considering rollups too
- direct writers (geico, progressive direct) eating personal lines from below

risks
- coastal property mkts — wind/flood carrier appetite has tightened 3 yrs running. citizens (FL state pool) and similar GA pools are growing. if the carriers further pull back, henry's specialization becomes a liability not an asset.
- the marina-group account — 8% of revenue, one decision-maker, and that decision-maker is henry's poker buddy. transition risk is real
- hard market is helping all P&C agencies right now. if rates soften in 2 yrs, ebitda growth disappears
- producer succession. henry's $620k book has to be assigned to other producers. will keller can only absorb so much

upsides
- coastal commercial niche is hard to replicate, real specialization
- nephew is the right successor — young, hungry, has the relationships
- a few wins on new commercial accounts could replace any retention loss

thesis
this is a solid tuck-in for a regional p&c platform. its not a platform itself — too small + too geographically concentrated. concentration risks (carriers, marina account, henry's personal book) are addressable but real.

would i pay 2.5x? yes. 3x? no, too rich for what we get.
