oakwood funeral group - 1/15 visit

this one is sad. the founder (paul mancuso) died of a heart attack in november. now its an estate sale, run by his widow brenda and her brother-in-law tony who's been the GM at the main location since 2008. brenda doesnt want to run it, the kids dont want it (both in tech in austin), tony cant afford to buy it. so a sale is the only option.

setup
- 3 funeral home locations, mid-michigan, all within a 30 mile radius
- 2 of the 3 also have on-site cemetery, ~14 acres developed + ~22 acres undeveloped at the main location
- 4th location was sold in 2019 in a separate transaction
- ~620 calls/yr combined across 3 locations (call = a "case", one decedent)
- avg revenue per case ~$8,400 traditional service, $3,100 cremation. mix is 47% cremation, climbing.

financials (best we have right now, no QoE done)
- ttm rev: $5.6M
- ebitda: claimed $1.32M but paul was running a lot of personal stuff thru the biz, real number after addbacks is probably $1.5-1.6M
- preneed trust assets: $3.8M (this is liability matching not asset, but the float matters)
- cemetery merchandise trust: $1.1M

the real value isn't the going concern ebitda. its
1. the cemetery land — 22 undeveloped acres @ ~$45k/acre developed value = potentially $7M+ over 15+ yrs as inventory is sold
2. the preneed contracts as a forward stream of cases (decedents who pre-paid for services that will be performed in the future)
3. brand + location (these homes have been in their communities 40-60 years)

valuation framing
- going concern: 6.5-7.5x ebitda for funeral, so $9.5-12M based on $1.5M normalized
- but the cemetery land + preneed asset value is on top of that
- comp set: SCI (Service Corp International) and StoneMor / Park Lawn are the public consolidators. they've been buying cemetery+funeral combos at 8-9x for cleaner properties.
- estate is asking $14M including real estate. broker (matthews holman) thinks $12.5-13M is doable.

risks (lots)
- ESTATE SALE — the widow has no expertise. could be slow, could come w/ surprises. probate complications possible. need our atty looking at this carefully.
- tony is the operator. if tony walks the day after close we have 3 funeral homes and no FD-license-holder running them. MI requires a licensed funeral director as a managing officer per location. need tony locked in HARD before signing.
- cremation rate climbing nationally — 60% by 2030 projected. cremation is lower-margin than traditional. their mix is already at 47% which is below national avg, but moving up. revenue per case erodes.
- preneed actuarial — when did paul last revalue the preneed book? if pricing was set 10 yrs ago for services delivered today, the margin is gone or negative. KEY DILIGENCE ITEM.
- cemetery land — need to confirm zoning, water rights, any environmental restrictions. 22 acres of cemetery land sounds like a great optionality but we have to be sure we can actually sell plots there
- competition: 4 other funeral homes within 25 mi. SCI has one of them. SCI may also be looking at this same deal — broker hasnt confirmed if he's shopping it broadly.

thesis
the funeral industry consolidates well. recurring "demand" is unfortunately reliable. paul's group has location density (3 funeral homes in a tight radius supports one prep center, one fleet of cars, shared mgmt — synergy is real). the cemetery land is the kicker — most independent funeral homes don't have undeveloped cemetery inventory, this is unusual.

main risks are deal-specific (estate process, tony retention) not industry-specific.

would offer $10.5-11M cash + 2 yr earnout on case volume retention + 5% rollover for tony (he needs to be locked up).

next steps
- engage funeral-industry QoE (Johnson Consulting Group or McSherry & Hudson, both have done these for us)
- atty review of paul's estate situation
- preneed actuarial review (independent)
- zoning + environmental on cemetery land
- 1:1 meeting w/ tony, sound out his terms / future
- check if SCI or Park Lawn is also in this deal
- comp pull on recent funeral home transactions in MI/OH/IN
